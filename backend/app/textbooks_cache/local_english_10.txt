LESSON FORTY-TWO 
READING: NATIONAL HEROES 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skill: Reading, Speaking, Critical Thinking 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to read and understand texts about 
national heroes, identify general and specific information, explain why national 
heroes are important for their country, understand new vocabulary from context, 
and express personal opinions clearly using full sentences. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Predicting the Topic (42.1) 
At the beginning of the lesson the teacher shows a picture of a famous national 
hero. The teacher asks students who the person might be and what they know about 
him or her. Students predict that the text will be about national heroes, history, 
leadership, and important people of the country. This activity prepares students to 
understand the main ideas of the reading. 
III. Reading 
Topic: What Is a National Hero? 
Students read a text about the meaning of national heroes. They learn that national 
heroes are people who did important things for their country and people. Students 
understand that heroes can be political leaders, writers, scientists, or social activists 
who worked for independence, culture, and progress. They also learn why people 
respect national heroes. 
IV. Reading Comprehension 
Topic: Understanding Key Ideas (42.2) 
Students answer questions about what a national hero is, why people respect 
national heroes, and how national heroes influence society. Students are 
encouraged to answer using full sentences and information from the text. 
V. Vocabulary in Context 
Topic: Understanding New Words (42.3) 
Students read a short text and choose the correct meaning of the word independent 
based on context. They also match the word independent with its correct definition. 
Students understand that independent means being free and making one’s own 
decisions. 
VI. Reading 
Topic: Shirinsho Shotemur 
Students read the text about Shirinsho Shotemur. They learn that he was an 
important national hero of Tajikistan who played a key role in the formation of the 
Tajik state. Students understand how he defended the rights, culture, and future of 
the Tajik people. 
VII. Reading for General Understanding 
Topic: Main Ideas (42.4)

Students answer general questions about who Shirinsho Shotemur was and why he 
is considered a national hero. They identify the main idea of the text. 
VIII. Reading for Specific Information 
Topic: Detailed Questions (42.5) 
Students answer detailed questions about Shirinsho Shotemur’s activities, 
achievements, and importance for the Tajik people. They explain why his work 
was important for the country. 
IX. Critical Thinking 
Topic: Evaluating the Writer’s Ideas (42.6) 
Students answer questions for themselves about whether they agree with the idea 
that national heroes are important and whether everyone would choose the same 
heroes. They explain their opinions and suggest what other information could be 
added to the text. 
X. Pair Discussion 
Topic: Heroes in Modern Society (42.7) 
Students discuss questions with a partner. They talk about who their favorite 
national hero is, what qualities make a person a hero, and whether modern society 
still needs heroes. Students listen carefully to their partner and respond politely. 
XI. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what new information they 
learned about national heroes. Students share short reflections using complete 
sentences, and the teacher gives brief feedback on reading and speaking skills. 
XII. Homework 
Students write a short paragraph about a national hero they know. They explain 
who the person is, what he or she did for the country, and why they respect this 
person. 
Vocabulary (10 words – English / Tajik) 
1. national hero — қаҳрамони миллӣ 
2. independence — истиқлолият 
3. leader — роҳбар 
4. history — таърих 
5. culture — фарҳанг 
6. rights — ҳуқуқ 
7. society — ҷомеа 
8. respect — эҳтиром 
9. achievement — дастовард 
10. future — оянда

LESSON FORTY-THREE 
READING: HERO OF TAJIKISTAN – SHIRINSHO SHOTEMUR 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skills: Reading, Speaking, Critical Thinking 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to read and understand a biographical 
text about Shirinsho Shotemur, identify general and specific information, answer 
factual and personal questions, explain historical events in simple English, and 
express their own opinions using full sentences. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Predicting the Topic (43.1) 
At the beginning of the lesson the teacher shows a picture of Shirinsho Shotemur 
or writes his name on the board. The teacher asks students what they know about 
him and whether they have heard his name before. Students predict that the text 
will be about a national hero, history, and the formation of Tajikistan. This activity 
prepares students for reading. 
III. Reading 
Topic: Hero of Tajikistan – Shirinsho Shotemur 
Students read the text about Shirinsho Shotemur. They learn about his birth, 
childhood, education, work experience, and political activity. Students understand 
that he was born into a poor family, worked from a young age, learned several 
languages, and played an important role in the creation of the Tajik Soviet Socialist 
Republic. 
IV. Reading for General Understanding 
Topic: Main Ideas (43.2) 
Students answer general questions such as who Shirinsho Shotemur was and why 
he is remembered today. They identify the main idea of the text and understand its 
overall meaning. 
V. Reading for Specific Information 
Topic: Detailed Questions (43.3) 
Students read the text again and answer the following questions using full 
sentences: 
a. When was Shirinsho born? 
b. Where was he born? 
c. When he was born, was Tajikistan an independent country? 
d. Was he born into a rich family? 
e. How did he learn to speak Russian? 
f. What other language did he learn to speak? 
g. Which political party did he join? 
h. What happened to Tajikistan in 1929? 
VI. Vocabulary Work 
Topic: Words from the Text (43.4)

The teacher explains key words from the text such as independent, political leader, 
party, republic, empire. Students use some of the words in their own sentences. 
VII. Personal Response 
Topic: Thinking about History (43.5) 
Students answer the questions for themselves and discuss them briefly: 
a. Do you think that Tajikistan changed during Shirinsho's time? Why or why not? 
b. Why do you think Shirinsho wanted Tajikistan to be independent? 
c. Do you think it was hard growing up in Shirinsho's time? Why or why not? 
VIII. Pair Discussion 
Topic: National Heroes (43.6) 
Students work in pairs and discuss who they think is a national hero today and 
what qualities a hero should have. They are encouraged to listen carefully and 
respond politely. 
IX. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what new information they 
learned about Shirinsho Shotemur and the history of Tajikistan. Students share 
short reflections, and the teacher gives feedback on reading comprehension and 
speaking skills. 
X. Homework 
Students write a short paragraph (8–10 sentences) about Shirinsho Shotemur or 
another national hero. They explain who the person is, what he or she did for the 
country, and why they respect this person.

LESSON ONE 
LISTENING: FESTIVALS 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skills: Listening, Speaking, Critical Thinking 
Students can listen for general and specific information. 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to listen for general and specific information, 
predict content using pictures, understand information about festivals in different countries, 
express personal opinions about celebrations, and answer questions using full sentences. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Talking about Pictures (1.1) 
Students look at the second picture and discuss the questions: 
What do you see? What do you think they are celebrating? Why? 
How would you feel if you met these people at a celebration? 
How do people celebrate in Tajikistan? How do people celebrate in your area? 
Do people celebrate in different ways in different parts of Tajikistan? How do they celebrate? 
This activity activates students’ background knowledge and prepares them for listening. 
III. Pre-listening 
Topic: Personal Questions (1.2) 
Students complete the questions and answer them for themselves: 
What is your favorite celebration? 
Why is it your favorite celebration? 
What do you do during this celebration? 
Students may share answers with a partner. 
IV. Pre-listening 
Topic: Predicting from Pictures (1.3) 
Students look at the second picture again and predict answers to the questions: 
What are they celebrating? 
Do men and women celebrate together? 
How do they celebrate? 
Do they give gifts? 
How is this different from where you live? 
Do you think this is a nice celebration? 
V. Listening 
Topic: Checking Predictions (1.4) 
Students listen to the recording and check their answers from Activity 1.3. 
The teacher plays the recording one or two times if necessary. 
VI. Listening 
Topic: Festivals Around the World (1.5) 
Students look at the pictures, listen to the recording, and answer the questions: 
Which country is each festival from? 
What is the name of each festival?

What is your opinion of each festival? 
Would you like to attend any of these festivals? 
N.B. Chinese New Year is a New Year celebration in China and many other Asian countries, 
usually at the end of January or the beginning of February. 
VII. Listening for Specific Information 
Topic: Completing the Table (1.6) 
Students listen again and complete the table with information about Festival 1, Festival 2, and 
Festival 3: 
What does the festival celebrate? 
How long is the festival? 
Why does the speaker like the festival? 
Who takes part in the festival? 
VIII. Post-listening 
Topic: Personal Response (1.7) 
Students answer the questions for themselves: 
Why do you think these celebrations are popular? 
Think of three reasons why they are popular. 
Which festival would you most like to attend and why? 
Which festival would you NOT like to attend and why? 
Students discuss their answers in pairs or small groups. 
IX. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what new information they learned about 
festivals. Students give short answers using full sentences. The teacher gives brief feedback on 
listening and speaking skills. 
X. Homework 
Students write a short paragraph (8–10 sentences) about a festival they like. They explain what 
the festival celebrates, how people celebrate it, and why it is popular.

LESSON TWO 
VOCABULARY: BODY LANGUAGE 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skills: Vocabulary, Reading, Speaking, Critical Thinking 
Students can understand and use vocabulary related to body language and explain meanings 
using examples. 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to understand common body language verbs, match 
words with definitions, use new vocabulary in sentences, read and understand a text about body 
language, identify key ideas, and express personal opinions using full sentences. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Introduction to Body Language (2.1) 
The teacher asks students: 
How do we communicate without words? 
Do people use body language when they meet others? 
Students then match the definitions with the verbs: 
to nod 
to fold your arms 
to hold hands 
to cover your hair/head 
to wave 
to wink 
to bow 
to look someone in the eye 
to hug 
Students match the verbs with the correct definitions and check answers together. 
III. Vocabulary Practice 
Topic: Grouping the Verbs (2.2) 
Students put the verbs into the correct boxes: 
When you meet a friend 
When you meet a stranger 
To communicate with body language 
Students discuss their answers. 
Then the teacher asks students to translate the words into Tajik. 
IV. Vocabulary in Context 
Topic: Using the Words in Sentences (2.3) 
Students read the sentences and put the new words in the spaces. 
They change the form of the verb if needed: 
to nod 
to fold your arms 
to shake hands 
to cover your hair/head 
to wave 
to wink 
to bow 
to look someone in the eye 
to hug 
Students complete all sentences and check answers with the class. 
V. Reading 
Topic: What Is Body Language? 
Students read the text about body language. 
They learn that people communicated with body language before modern language developed. 
They understand that body language includes facial expressions, movement, posture, and touch. 
Students also learn that body language can be different in different cultures and is very important 
in communication, jobs, and everyday life.

VI. Reading Comprehension 
Topic: Understanding the Text (2.4) 
Students answer the questions: 
What is body language? 
Do people use the same body language in different parts of the world? 
Why is body language important? 
Students answer using information from the text. 
VII. Vocabulary from the Text 
Topic: New Words (2.5) 
Students read the text again and identify any new or difficult words. 
The teacher explains the meanings and students write them in their notebooks. 
VIII. Personal Response 
Topic: Talking about Yourself (2.6) 
Students answer the questions for themselves: 
Do you do any of these things yourself? When and why? 
What other physical communication do people use in Tajikistan? 
Does your community communicate in a way that other communities do not? 
Students share ideas orally. 
IX. Critical Thinking 
Topic: Using Body Language (2.7) 
Students discuss the best way to communicate these messages using body language: 
to show love 
to show agreement 
to show respect 
to show that you are serious 
to say hello 
to communicate secretly 
to show trust and honesty 
to hide a part of yourself or to show respect 
Students explain their answers. 
X. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what new words they learned and why body 
language is important. 
The teacher gives brief feedback on vocabulary use, reading, and speaking. 
XI. Homework 
Students write a short paragraph (8–10 sentences) about body language in Tajikistan. 
They explain which types of body language people use, when they use them, and why they are 
important.

LESSON THREE 
GRAMMAR: ORDERING EVENTS 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skills: Grammar, Reading, Writing, Speaking 
Students can identify and use connecting words to order events. 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to recognize and use connecting 
words, put events in the correct order, choose appropriate connectors, and write a 
short story using time and sequence words correctly. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Talking about Events 
The teacher asks students what words they use to talk about events in order. 
Students give examples such as first, then, after, when. This prepares students for 
the grammar topic. 
III. Grammar Presentation 
Topic: Ordering Events (3.1) 
Students read the text. The sentences are not in the correct order. They put the 
sentences in the correct order using connecting words. 
Three days ago, when I was coming home from work, I met an old friend. 
We stopped and had a coffee while we chatted. 
He told me about everything he did since college. 
First of all, he got married when he was 22, as soon as he graduated college. 
Secondly, he had two children, a boy and a girl, and he would like to have a third 
child, another girl. 
As soon as we finished our coffee I had to go. 
Afterwards, I went to the market and bought things for dinner. 
It was very nice to catch up with him while on my way home. 
IV. Grammar Understanding 
Topic: Talking about the Text (3.2) 
Students read the text again and answer the questions. 
What connects the sentences and actions? 
Are there words that help you understand the order? 
Students answer using examples from the text. 
V. Grammar Practice 
Topic: Grouping Connecting Words (3.3) 
Students put the words into the correct boxes. 
When, as soon as, then, afterwards, after, before, while, just as, first(ly), 
second(ly), third(ly), to begin with, then after that, finally, eventually 
General time and sequence 
At the same time 
Specific sequence 
One thing after another 
Students check answers together.

VI. Grammar Practice 
Topic: Choosing the Correct Word (3.4) 
Students read the sentences and choose the correct option. 
I will cook dinner when I am ready. 
Muyassar will be here as soon as he finishes work. 
I will meet you after the football match. 
Well, when baking a cake, to begin with, the first thing I do is turn the oven on. 
VII. Writing 
Topic: Creating a Story (3.5) 
Students create an interesting story about themselves. They use connecting words 
to order events. They choose ideas such as the winning ticket, an unexpected 
meeting, the worst day ever, a new friend, the big game, a special meal, time to 
celebrate, a dangerous mistake, the great escape, or our little secret. Students write 
8–10 sentences using words like first, then, after, while, finally. 
VIII. Speaking 
Topic: Sharing Stories 
Some students read their stories aloud. The class listens and identifies the 
connecting words used. 
IX. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what new grammar words they 
learned and how connecting words help make a story clear. The teacher gives brief 
feedback on grammar and writing. 
X. Homework 
Students write a short paragraph about an event in their life using at least five 
connecting words. 
VOCABULARY (Grammar Words) 
first — аввал 
then — баъд 
after — пас аз 
before — пеш аз 
while — ҳамзамон 
as soon as — ҳамин ки 
finally — дар охир 
eventually — оқибат

LESSON FOUR 
GRAMMAR: CONTRASTING 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skills: Grammar, Reading, Writing, Speaking 
Students can identify and use connecting words to contrast ideas. 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to understand the use of contrasting 
connecting words, join different ideas using although, though, even though, in spite 
of, despite, compare opinions, and write a short paragraph expressing contrast 
using full sentences. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Different Ideas 
The teacher asks students if two ideas can be true at the same time even if they are 
different. Students give simple examples. This introduces the idea of contrast. 
III. Grammar Presentation 
Topic: Using “Although” (4.1) 
Students match the sentences (1–5) to the sentences in the box and use “although” 
to connect them. They remember that “although” can come at the beginning or in 
the middle of a sentence. The first sentence is already completed. 
Although we don’t speak the same language, both our languages come from 
Persian. 
Although technology makes our lives easier, it also brings a lot of changes and can 
affect our culture a lot. 
Although other cultures can seem very different, the differences between us mean 
we can learn from each other. 
Although I speak Tajik and he speaks Dari, we managed to communicate well. 
Although there are different people with different cultures in our country, we are 
all Tajik. 
IV. Grammar Rules 
Topic: Understanding the Rules (4.2) 
Students read the rules and choose the correct options. 
Although is a connecting word. 
Although joins two clauses. 
Although is used to join different ideas. 
Although can be used at the beginning or in the middle of the sentence. 
If although is used at the beginning of the sentence, there is a comma in the 
middle. 
V. Grammar Practice 
Topic: Completing Sentences (4.3) 
Students read the sentences and complete them using the correct connecting words: 
although, though, even though, in spite of, despite. 
It is important for us to respect other cultures even though we are often very 
different.

I think it is also important to learn other languages although it can be difficult. 
I learn about my culture from my family and community despite modern 
influences. 
I sometimes find it hard to study literature in school though I enjoy reading. 
VI. Reading and Thinking 
Topic: Comparing Opinions (4.4) 
Students look at the essay question about culture and change. They read Opinion A 
and Opinion B. 
They answer the questions. 
Do you agree more with Opinion A or Opinion B? 
Why would someone choose Opinion A or Opinion B? 
In pairs, students add their own ideas or examples to the boxes. 
VII. Writing 
Topic: Comparing Ideas in a Paragraph (4.5) 
Students write one paragraph comparing the two ideas. They use connecting words 
such as although, though, even though, despite, and in spite of. They include ideas 
from the discussion and their own opinions. 
VIII. Speaking 
Topic: Sharing Opinions 
Some students read their paragraphs aloud. Other students listen and identify the 
contrasting words used. 
IX. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what new grammar words they 
learned and how contrasting words help express ideas clearly. The teacher gives 
brief feedback on grammar and writing. 
X. Homework 
Students write a short paragraph about tradition and modern life using at least three 
contrasting connecting words. 
VOCABULARY (Grammar Words) 
although — гарчанде 
though — ҳарчанд 
even though — бо вуҷуди он ки 
despite — сарфи назар аз 
in spite of — бо вуҷуди 
contrast — муқоиса, фарқият 
culture — фарҳанг 
tradition — анъана

LESSON SIX 
READING: TRADITIONAL GAMES 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skills: Reading, Speaking, Critical Thinking 
Students can read for specific information and general meaning. 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to read and understand a text about traditional 
nomadic games, identify general and specific information, explain why traditional games are 
important for culture, answer factual and personal questions, translate key vocabulary, and 
express opinions using full sentences. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Looking at Pictures (6.1) 
Students look at the picture and answer the questions. 
Where do you think this is? 
What are the people playing? 
Do you have similar games in your own community? 
This activity prepares students for the reading topic. 
III. Reading 
Topic: Traditional Games in the Tajik Mountains (6.2) 
Students read the text about Savrinoz from Gorno-Badakhshan. 
They learn about nomadic life in the Pamir mountains, how nomads move with animals, and how 
traditional games with horses are part of their culture. 
Students understand that although many people live modern lives today, nomadic traditions and 
games still exist and are important. 
IV. Reading for General Understanding 
Topic: Choosing Titles (6.3) 
Students read the text again and choose the best title for each paragraph. 
They match paragraphs with the correct titles: 
Life in the Tajik Mountains: Old and New. 
A Wild Life! 
Globalizing Nomads. 
Building Cultural Bridges. 
V. Reading for Specific Information 
Topic: Answering Questions (6.4) 
Students answer the questions using full sentences. 
Where is Savrinoz from? 
Are all Tajik people today nomadic? 
Why do nomads take their sheep down to lower places in winter? 
What types of sport do they practice and why do they use horses? 
Are these nomadic games only for nomadic people? 
Why does Savrinoz want the games to continue? 
VI. Personal Response 
Topic: Thinking about Traditions (6.5) 
Students answer the questions for themselves. 
Do you think most Tajik people like these games? Why or why not? 
Do you think it is important for Tajik people to keep their traditions? Why or why not? 
Do you have any traditional games or traditions in your community that are important to you? 
Why or why not?

VII. Vocabulary Work 
Topic: Words from the Text (6.6) 
In pairs, students choose five words in bold from the text and translate them into Tajik. 
Students discuss whether they use these words in everyday life. 
They test each other on the meanings of the words. 
VIII. Critical Thinking 
Topic: Understanding Feelings and Values (6.7) 
Students read the text again and answer the questions for themselves. 
Does Savrinoz care about her community and culture? How do you know? 
Which parts of the text show how she feels? 
Do you think she puts her own needs first or the needs of other people first? 
Should we think about other people when we make decisions? 
What is important to you about your community and culture? Explain. 
IX. Speaking 
Topic: Sharing Ideas 
Students share some of their answers orally. The teacher encourages respectful listening and full 
sentences. 
X. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what new information they learned about 
nomadic life and traditional games. The teacher gives brief feedback on reading comprehension 
and speaking skills. 
XI. Homework 
Topic: Community Project (6.8) 
Students look for three examples of people in their community who put other people first when 
making decisions. 
They write what these people did and what they might have done differently if they had put 
themselves first.

III. Writing Focus 
Topic: Topic Sentences 
The teacher explains that topic sentences go at the start of a paragraph and tell the reader what 
the paragraph is about. 
IV. Rules for Topic Sentences (8.1) 
Students read the rules for topic sentences. 
Do not add new ideas unconnected to your essay topic. 
Do not include any sub-topics in your topic sentence. 
Keep the topic sentence short. 
Say what you are going to discuss. 
Connect the new paragraph with the previous one. 
V. Reading and Understanding 
Topic: Identifying the Topic (8.2) 
Students read the text about life in Nurek and answer the question. 
What is the text about? 
Students understand that the paragraph is about memories of life in Nurek and the difference

VI. Writing Practice 
Topic: Creating a Topic Sentence (8.3) 
Students read the second text and create a suitable topic sentence for it. 
They understand that the paragraph compares life in Dushanbe and Nurek and focuses on daily 
life and convenience. 
VII. Choosing an Opinion 
Topic: Essay Titles (8.4)

Dinner should always include meat.

Topic: Essay Structure (8.5 – 8.6)

X. Self-check

LESSON EIGHTEEN 
WRITING: COMPARE & CONTRAST 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skills: Writing, Reading, Critical Thinking 
Students can compare and contrast in writing. 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to understand a compare-and-contrast text, identify 
topic sentences, recognize similarities and differences, identify examples that support ideas, and 
understand how compare-and-contrast texts are organized. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Predicting from a Picture (18.1) 
Students look at the picture and answer the questions. 
Where was the picture taken? 
What do you think the people are doing there and why? 
What do you think the text will be about? 
Students share predictions about exploration, travel, and discovery. 
III. Reading 
Topic: Exploration – Past and Present 
Students read the text about exploration in the past and today. 
They understand that the text compares how exploration has changed over time and how some 
things have stayed the same. 
Students notice that each paragraph starts with a topic sentence. 
IV. Reading Skills 
Topic: Topic Sentences (18.2) 
Students look back at the text and match each topic sentence to the correct paragraph. 
They identify the main idea of each paragraph before reading the details. 
V. Text Organization 
Topic: Compare and Contrast Structure (18.3) 
Students read the text again and decide which type of organization is used. 
They choose between: 
Option 1 – Introduction, differences in the past, differences today, similarities. 
Option 2 – Introduction, similarities, differences. 
Students discuss why the writer chose this structure. 
VI. Reading for Detail 
Topic: Examples in the Text (18.4) 
Students read the text again and identify examples that show comparison and contrast. 
They underline phrases such as in contrast, however, similarly, in the same way, even though. 
They explain how these examples support the main idea of each paragraph. 
VII. Writing Focus 
Topic: Comparing and Contrasting 
The teacher explains that each paragraph should have one main idea and more than one example. 
Students discuss why examples are important in compare-and-contrast writing. 
VIII. Guided Practice 
Topic: Talking about Similarities and Differences 
Students work in pairs and compare two things they know, such as life in the city and life in the 
countryside, old homes and new homes, or school today and school in the past. 
They list similarities and differences.

IX. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what they learned about compare-and-contrast 
writing and which words help show similarities and differences. 
The teacher gives brief feedback on reading and thinking skills. 
X. Homework 
Students write one paragraph (8–10 sentences) comparing and contrasting two things of their 
choice. 
They use at least four connecting words such as however, in contrast, similarly, and even though.

contrast — фарқият нишон додан 
similar — монанд 
different — гуногун 
example — мисол 
topic sentence — ҷумлаи мавзӯӣ

exploration — кашф

LESSON NINETEEN 
WRITING: PRACTICE 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skills: Writing, Critical Thinking, Organization 
Students can write essays that compare and contrast. 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to organize ideas for a compare-and-contrast essay, 
identify similarities and differences between two topics, write clear topic sentences, support 
ideas with examples, use connecting words correctly, and write a draft essay. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Reviewing Compare and Contrast 
The teacher asks students what it means to compare and contrast. 
Students give examples such as old and new homes or city and village life. 
This refreshes knowledge from Lesson Eighteen. 
III. Writing Focus 
Topic: Organizing Ideas (19.1) 
Students read the rules. 
The teacher explains that a compare-and-contrast essay compares two things that are similar in 
some ways and different in other ways. 
Good organization helps the reader understand ideas clearly. 
IV. Planning 
Topic: Using a Diagram (19.2) 
Students complete the diagram with their own ideas. 
They compare homes in Tajikistan with homes in other countries. 
They write differences on each side and similarities in the middle. 
Students may discuss ideas in pairs before writing. 
V. Writing Preparation 
Topic: Topic Sentences (19.3) 
Students choose three main points. 
One point shows a similarity. 
Two points show differences. 
They write three topic sentences, one for each paragraph. 
VI. Supporting Ideas 
Topic: Examples (19.4) 
Students add three supporting points under each topic sentence. 
Each supporting point gives an example or explanation. 
This helps make the paragraph clear and detailed. 
VII. Language Focus 
Topic: Connecting Words (19.5) 
Students look back at connecting words from Unit 01. 
They choose different connecting words to link their paragraphs. 
They try not to repeat the same connector more than once. 
VIII. Writing 
Topic: Writing a Draft Essay (19.6) 
Students write a draft of their compare-and-contrast essay. 
They include an introduction, body paragraphs with topic sentences and examples, and a short 
conclusion.

IX. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what was easy and what was difficult about 
writing the essay. 
The teacher gives brief feedback on organization, clarity, and use of connecting words. 
X. Homework 
Students revise their draft essay and write a clean final version. 
They check topic sentences, supporting ideas, and connecting words.

LESSON FIFTY 
GRAMMAR: THE ZERO CONDITIONAL 
UNIT 06 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skills: Grammar, Reading, Writing, Speaking, Critical Thinking 
Students can identify and use the zero conditional. 
 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to understand what the zero conditional is, 
recognize zero conditional sentences in context, correctly match causes and results, use 
the zero conditional to talk about general truths and real situations, complete sentences 
accurately, and write a short letter using zero conditional sentences to talk about 
protecting the environment. 
 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Observing Cause and Result 
Students look carefully at the picture and answer the question orally. 
They explain what is happening in the picture and describe the actions and results they 
can see. 
The teacher guides students to notice that actions often have natural results, such as 
cleaning, protecting, or damaging the environment. 
Students speak in full sentences and give simple examples from real life. 
 
III. Introducing the Zero Conditional 
Topic: Understanding Cause and Effect 
The teacher explains that we use the zero conditional to talk about things that are always 
true or usually true. 
The structure is explained clearly: If / When + present simple, present simple. 
Students are told that we use the zero conditional to talk about facts, habits, rules, and 
scientific truths, especially about the environment and daily life. 
 
IV. Grammar Practice: Matching Sentences (50.2) 
Topic: Matching Causes and Results 
Students match the first half of each sentence with the correct second half. 
They carefully think about which result always happens when the first action happens. 
Students explain their choices orally, for example why protecting forests helps trees grow 
or why destroying forests causes plants and animals to die. 
The teacher checks answers and reinforces the idea of cause and result. 
 
V. Reading and Grammar in Context (50.3) 
Topic: The Environment in 2050 
Students read the text slowly and carefully from beginning to end. 
They focus on understanding the message about climate change and the future of the 
planet. 
Students then fill in the spaces by choosing the correct answers (a, b, or c). 
They pay attention to verb forms and notice that the zero conditional uses the present 
simple in both parts of the sentence. 
The teacher explains why each correct answer fits the zero conditional rule.

VI. Grammar Explanation 
Topic: How the Zero Conditional Works 
The teacher clearly explains that the zero conditional is used when the result always 
happens if the condition happens. 
Examples are discussed, such as: 
If we cut down forests, animals lose their homes. 
When people burn fuels, the air becomes polluted. 
Students repeat the structure and identify the “if/when clause” and the result clause. 
 
VII. Controlled Writing Practice (50.4) 
Topic: Completing Zero Conditional Sentences 
Students complete the sentences using their own ideas but keeping the zero conditional 
structure. 
They write full, clear sentences and think about real situations such as traffic, shopping, 
health, and the environment. 
Students check their answers in pairs and help each other correct mistakes. 
 
VIII. Writing for a Real Purpose (50.5) 
Topic: Writing a Letter to Help the Environment 
Students write a letter explaining how their school can help the environment. 
They include ideas such as keeping the school clean, saving water, planting trees, and 
reducing plastic. 
They must explain what happens if the school makes positive changes and what happens 
if it does not. 
Students are encouraged to use several zero conditional sentences to clearly show cause 
and effect. 
 
IX. Reflection and Discussion 
The teacher asks students to give examples of zero conditional sentences about their own 
lives or environment. 
Students explain why the zero conditional is useful when talking about rules, habits, and 
environmental problems. 
The teacher gives detailed feedback on grammar use and clarity of ideas. 
 
X. Homework 
Students write 6–8 zero conditional sentences about daily life, school rules, or 
environmental protection, making sure each sentence clearly shows cause and result.

They then think more deeply and discuss why people argue, explaining that arguments often 
happen because people have different opinions, emotions, experiences, or because they do not 
listen carefully to each other. 
Students also discuss whether people always listen to each other’s opinions and explain that

Students are encouraged to think about times when a small misunderstanding caused a big

IV. Reading: Avoiding Problems with Communication 
Students read the text slowly and carefully, focusing on meaning rather than speed. 
The teacher explains that the text is about how to avoid miscommunication and how to speak and 
listen in a positive and calm way. 
Main Ideas of the Text (Explained Simply): 
The text explains that good communication needs calm behavior and careful thinking before 
speaking. 
It shows that listening carefully is more important than speaking quickly and emotionally. 
The text also teaches that when we disagree with someone, we should first respect their opinion 
and then explain our own ideas politely. 
Finally, the text explains that communication is not only about words but also about 
understanding the person we are speaking to.

1. to avoid → f) When you stop yourself from doing something or stop something bad from

2. half listening → a) When you don’t listen carefully to the other person. 
3. listening carefully → d) When you listen carefully to what someone is saying. 
4. showing emotion → b) When someone can see your feelings. 
5. to disagree → c) When you have a different opinion. 
6. bad reaction → e) When someone shows that they don’t like something or gets angry.

Finally, students explain how we can communicate better without causing negative reactions,

opinions.

V. Listening (Second time) 
Topic: Understanding emotions (72.3) 
Хонандагон бори дуюм сабтро мешунаванд. 
Онҳо муайян мекунанд, ки Ted чӣ гуна эҳсос дорад ва чаро. 
Муаллим мепурсад, ки оё Chloe дӯсти хуб аст ва чӣ гуна ин дар суҳбат маълум мешавад. 
VI. Language Focus 
Topic: Useful Language Box (72.4 – 72.5) 
Хонандагон ибораҳоро ба се гурӯҳ ҷудо мекунанд: 
– эҳсосоти мусбат 
– эҳсосоти манфӣ 
– саволҳо 
Муаллим маънои ҳар ибораро мефаҳмонад ва мисол меорад. 
VII. Reading 
Topic: Ways to feel better (72.6) 
Хонандагон матнро мехонанд. 
Онҳо мефаҳманд, ки чӣ гуна иваз кардани ҷой, суҳбат кардан, навиштан ва медитатсия 
метавонад ба беҳтар шудани ҳолати равонӣ кӯмак кунад. 
Саволҳоро ҷавоб медиҳанд ва фикри шахсии худро мегӯянд. 
VIII. Pair Work (Speaking) 
Topic: Writing a conversation (72.7) 
Хонандагон дар ҷуфтҳо кор мекунанд. 
Яке нақши шахси ғамгинро мебозад, дигаре кӯмак мекунад. 
Онҳо ибораҳои Useful Language Box-ро истифода мебаранд. 
Муаллим назорат мекунад ва кӯмак мерасонад. 
IX. Presentation 
Topic: Presenting the dialogue (72.8 – 72.9) 
Ҳар гурӯҳ суҳбати худро ба синф пешниҳод мекунад.

Хонандагони дигар гӯш мекунанд ва ҷадвалро пур мекунанд. 
Ба гурӯҳҳо аз 1 то 5 хол медиҳанд ва сабабашро мегӯянд. 
X. Reflection and Assessment 
Муаллим мепурсад, ки хонандагон имрӯз чӣ омӯхтанд. 
Онҳо мегӯянд, ки чӣ гуна метавонанд ба дӯстон дар ҳолати ғамгин кӯмак кунанд. 
Муаллим фикру иштирокро баҳогузорӣ мекунад.

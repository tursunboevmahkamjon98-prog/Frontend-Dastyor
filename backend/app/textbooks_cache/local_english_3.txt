Lesson 1: Greetings 
Competencies: a) Recognizes and uses basic greetings in English 
b) Introduces themselves and others with simple phrases 
c) Responds to basic questions like "How are you?" 
Learning Objectives: By the end of this lesson, students should be able to: 
a) Use greetings such as "Hello," "Hi," "Good morning," and "Goodbye" 
b) Say their name and ask for others' names 
c) Respond to "How are you?" with "I'm fine," or similar replies 
    Lesson Structure: 
Organizational Part: 
a) Greet students and check attendance 
b) Write the lesson topic "Greetings" on the board 
c) Ensure students are prepared for the lesson 
Review of Previous Knowledge: 
a) Ask students if they know any greetings in English 
b) Allow students to share what they know about saying "Hello" or "Goodbye" 
Introduction of New Topic: 
Introduce basic greetings and expressions used in English. Start with “Hello,” “Hi,” “Good morning,” 
“Good afternoon,” and “Goodbye.” Explain when each greeting is typically used, such as in the morning 
or afternoon, and in formal or informal settings. Practice each greeting, encouraging students to repeat 
after you. Then introduce responses like “I’m fine” or “Nice to meet you.” Discuss how we often ask 
“How are you?” to show interest in someone’s well-being, and respond appropriately. 
Example Table: 
Greeting Use 
Hello General greeting, any time of day 
Good morning Use in the morning 
Good afternoon Use in the afternoon 
Goodbye To end a conversation 
How are you? To ask about someone’s well-being 
I'm fine, thank you Common response to “How are you?” 
Activity: Pair Practice: 
Pair students and have them practice greetings and introductions. Each student should say “Hello, my 
name is...” and ask “What’s your name?” Encourage responses like “Nice to meet you.” 
Sentence Practice: 
Practice common phrases together, such as “Good morning, teacher,” and “Goodbye, class.” Have 
students take turns role-playing these exchanges. 
Conclusion: 
Summarize greetings and responses. Ask students to say “Hello” and “Goodbye” to each other, and 
encourage them to use these phrases with classmates outside of class. 
Homework: 
a) Practice saying “Hello” and “Goodbye” with family members or friends 
b) Learn the words and phrases on p. 13 
 
 Нақшаи яксоата аз фанни забони англисӣ синфи 3 Lesson 2: My Room 
Competencies: a) Identifies basic items found in a room 
b) Names and describes common objects in a simple sentence 
c) Uses "This is my..." to indicate possession 
Learning Objectives: By the end of this lesson, students should be able to: 
a) Recognize and name objects in a room (bed, table, chair, etc.) 
b) Use “This is my...” to talk about their belongings 
c) Make simple descriptions using colors (e.g., "This is a red chair")

Lesson Structure: 
Organizational Part: 
a) Greet students and review classroom rules 
b) Write “My Room” on the board 
c) Ensure students are ready to start 
Review of Previous Knowledge: 
a) Ask students to share their favorite things in their room 
b) Review greetings learned in Lesson 1 
Introduction of New Topic: 
Introduce vocabulary for items commonly found in a room, such as bed, table, chair, and lamp. Show 
pictures or point to similar items in the classroom if available. Explain how we can say “This is my...” to 
show what belongs to us. Practice saying “This is my bed,” “This is my table,” and so on. Encourage 
students to describe objects by color, like “This is a blue chair.” 
Example Table: 
Item Sentence Example 
Bed This is my bed 
Chair This is my chair 
Table This is my table 
Lamp This is my lamp 
Activity: Matching Game: 
Show pictures of different room items, and have students match the word to the item. Encourage them to 
say the sentences, “This is my (object).” 
Practice Sentences: 
Go over sentence structures like “This is a red bed” or “This is my green chair.” Let students practice 
creating simple sentences using these patterns. 
Conclusion: 
Recap the words learned and ask students to say a sentence about their favorite item in their room. 
Homework: 
a) Practice saying the names of objects in your room 
b) Learn the words on p. 14

Lesson 1: Greetings 
Competencies: a) Recognizes and uses basic greetings in English 
b) Introduces themselves and others with simple phrases 
c) Responds to basic questions like "How are you?" 
Learning Objectives: By the end of this lesson, students should be able to: 
a) Use greetings such as "Hello," "Hi," "Good morning," and "Goodbye" 
b) Say their name and ask for others' names 
c) Respond to "How are you?" with "I'm fine," or similar replies 
Lesson Structure:

1. Organizational Part: 
a) Greet students and check attendance 
b) Write the lesson topic "Greetings" on the board 
c) Ensure students are prepared for the lesson 
2. Review of Previous Knowledge: 
a) Ask students if they know any greetings in English 
b) Allow students to share what they know about saying "Hello" or "Goodbye" 
3. Introduction of New Topic: 
Introduce basic greetings and expressions used in English. Start with “Hello,” “Hi,” “Good 
morning,” “Good afternoon,” and “Goodbye.” Explain when each greeting is typically used, such 
as in the morning or afternoon, and in formal or informal settings. Practice each greeting, 
encouraging students to repeat after you. Then introduce responses like “I’m fine” or “Nice to 
meet you.” Discuss how we often ask “How are you?” to show interest in someone’s well-being, 
and respond appropriately. 
Example Table: 
Greeting Use 
Hello General greeting, any time of day 
Good morning Use in the morning 
Good afternoon Use in the afternoon 
Goodbye To end a conversation 
How are you? To ask about someone’s well-being 
I'm fine, thank you Common response to “How are you?” 
4. Activity: Pair Practice: 
Pair students and have them practice greetings and introductions. Each student should say “Hello, 
my name is...” and ask “What’s your name?” Encourage responses like “Nice to meet you.” 
5. Sentence Practice: 
Practice common phrases together, such as “Good morning, teacher,” and “Goodbye, class.” Have 
students take turns role-playing these exchanges. 
6. Conclusion: 
Summarize greetings and responses. Ask students to say “Hello” and “Goodbye” to each other, 
and encourage them to use these phrases with classmates outside of class. 
7. Homework: 
a) Practice saying “Hello” and “Goodbye” with family members or friends 
b) Learn the words and phrases on p. 13

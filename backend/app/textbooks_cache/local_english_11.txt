LESSON ONE 
Skill: Listening 
Topic: Personal Success 
Time: 45 minutes 
1. Learning outcomes 
Students can: 
 listen for general and specific information; 
 express opinions about success; 
 predict information before listening; 
 choose correct answers after listening. 
2. Competence 
Students learn to understand spoken information about success and discuss it using 
their own ideas. 
Салоҳият: 
Хонандагон иттилооти умумӣ ва мушаххасро аз матни шунавоӣ дарк намуда, 
фикри шахсии худро баён мекунанд. 
3. Teaching aids 
 Textbook 
 Pictures 
 Audio recording 
 Blackboard 
4. Procedure of the lesson 
I. Organizational moment (3 minutes) 
 Greeting 
 Checking attendance 
 Introducing the topic: “Personal Success” 
II. Lead-in / Speaking (7 minutes) 
Look at the picture and answer the questions: 
1. What is success? How do you define success? 
2. Do you know anyone who is successful? Who? How are they successful? 
Students answer freely. Teacher encourages discussion. 
III. Pair work – Speaking (8 minutes) 
1.2 Complete and discuss the sentences with your partner: 
1. The best thing I have achieved is… 
2. I am most proud of… 
3. I would love to… 
4. I hope to… 
Some students share their answers with the class. 
IV. Pre-listening (7 minutes) 
1.3 Look at the picture and predict the answers. 
Note: 
CEO = Chief Executive Officer (top manager of a company) 
Task: 
Put the statements in the order the speaker mentions them (1–5): 
a. Understanding the difference between needing and wanting 
b. Always trying your best 
c. Believing in yourself 
d. Having a home 
e. Having aims you can achieve

Students work individually, then compare answers. 
V. While-listening (10 minutes) 
1.4 Listen to the people talking about what makes people successful. 
 Were your answers correct or incorrect? 
 Write the correct order. 
Correct order (teacher checks): 
1. c – Believing in yourself 
2. b – Always trying your best 
3. e – Having aims you can achieve 
4. d – Having a home 
5. a – Understanding the difference between needing and wanting 
VI. Listening for detail (5 minutes) 
1.5 Listen again and answer the questions: 
1. Does the speaker think that success is all about making money? 
→ No, he doesn’t. 
2. Does the speaker think everyone should follow the same path to success? 
→ No, everyone has a different path. 
VII. Post-listening (5 minutes) 
1.6 Choose the correct answer: 
1. The interviewer thinks that: 
✅ c. success is different for each person. 
2. The speaker thinks that: 
✅ b. success is about focusing on both strengths and difficulties. 
3. The speaker thinks that aims are important because: 
✅ a. setting aims one at a time will make things easier to achieve. 
4. The speaker thinks that: 
✅ d. having people who support you and a place you love is success. 
5. The speaker thinks that: 
✅ d. enjoying life’s challenges and knowing what you want is the key to 
success. 
VIII. Consolidation (3 minutes) 
 What is success for you? 
 Is success the same for everyone? 
IX. Homework (2 minutes) 
 Write 5–6 sentences: “What success means to me” 
 Revise today’s listening answers 
X. Assessment 
Students are assessed according to: 
 listening comprehension; 
 participation in discussion; 
 correct answers.

LESSON THREE 
Grammar: Question Tags 
Time: 45 minutes 
1. Learning outcomes 
Students can: 
 identify question tags in spoken and written English; 
 understand how question tags are formed; 
 use question tags correctly in dialogues and sentences; 
 take part in short conversations using question tags. 
2. Competence 
Students develop grammatical competence by using question tags correctly to check 
information and keep conversations natural. 
Салоҳият: 
Хонандагон қоидаҳои question tags-ро аз худ намуда, онҳоро дар нутқи шифоҳӣ ва 
хаттӣ дуруст истифода мебаранд. 
3. Teaching aids 
 Textbook 
 Blackboard 
 Handouts 
4. Procedure of the lesson 
I. Organizational moment (3 minutes) 
 Greeting 
 Checking attendance 
 Introducing the topic: Question Tags 
II. Lead-in / Speaking (5 minutes) 
3.1 Look at the image. Answer the question: 
 What is happening in the image? 
Students describe the picture briefly. 
III. Pre-reading (5 minutes) 
3.2 Look at the title of the text. 
 What do you think the text is about? 
Teacher introduces the text “A Good Surprise”. 
IV. Reading (10 minutes) 
Students read the dialogue “A Good Surprise” silently or aloud. 
V. Comprehension check (5 minutes) 
3.3 Answer the questions: 
1. Why did the three friends meet? 
→ To plan a surprise party for Gemma. 
2. Who owns the restaurant they talk about? 
→ Brian’s uncle. 
3. How will they travel to the party? 
→ By cars of other guests. 
4. Do you like surprises? Why / why not? 
→ Personal answer. 
VI. Grammar focus: Question Tags (10 minutes) 
3.4 Work with your partner. Look at the examples and complete the rules. 
Correct rules: 
1. Question tags go at the end of a sentence. 
2. Question tags are mainly used in spoken English. 
3. We use question tags to check information we think we know. 
4. We put a comma before the question tag. 
5. If the initial statement is positive, the question tag is negative.

6. If the initial statement is negative, the question tag is positive. 
7. If the statement includes an auxiliary verb, use the auxiliary verb in the tag. 
8. If the statement includes a modal verb, use the modal verb in the tag. 
9. If there is no auxiliary or modal verb, use the verb needed to form a question. 
Teacher explains with examples. 
VII. Controlled practice (5 minutes) 
3.5 Choose the correct form 
Correct answers: 
 aren’t 
 isn’t 
 doesn’t 
 doesn’t 
 wouldn’t 
 wouldn’t 
 won’t 
 didn’t 
 didn’t 
 haven’t 
 do 
(Teacher checks orally.) 
VIII. Written practice (5 minutes) 
3.6 Complete the sentences using question tags 
1. You went there last year, didn’t you? 
2. We could watch a film at mine, couldn’t we? 
3. Horses are the best, aren’t they? 
4. You guys have met before, haven’t you? 
5. We’ll eat somewhere nicer tomorrow, won’t we? 
6. Your sister has that book, doesn’t she? 
7. It can’t be Sunday already, can it? 
8. You haven’t seen my glasses anywhere, have you? 
9. Michael doesn’t like mushrooms, does he? 
10. You didn’t forget to lock the door, did you? 
IX. Pair work (5 minutes) 
3.7 In pairs: 
 Write a short conversation between two friends planning the weekend. 
 Use at least 4 question tags. 
 Check grammar using rules from 3.4. 
Some pairs present their dialogues. 
5. Consolidation (2 minutes) 
 What are question tags used for? 
 Give one example. 
6. Homework (2 minutes) 
 Write 5 sentences with question tags 
 Prepare a short dialogue using question tags 
7. Assessment 
Students are assessed according to: 
 correct use of question tags; 
 participation in activities; 
 accuracy in speaking and writing.

LESSON FOUR

Topic: Jaloluddin Balkhi – Biography 
Time: 45 minutes

 understand cause-and-effect relationships in a biography; 
 answer comprehension questions;

2. Competence 
Students develop reading and thinking skills by identifying causes and effects in a

Салоҳият: 
Хонандагон матни тарҷумаиҳолиро хонда, робитаи сабаб ва натиҷаро дар 
ҳаёти шахс муайян мекунанд.

 Picture of Jaloluddin Balkhi 
 Blackboard 
4. Procedure of the lesson 
I. Organizational moment (3 minutes) 
 Greeting 
 Checking attendance 
 Introducing the topic: Cause and Effect Biography

4.1 Look at the picture and answer the questions: 
1. Who is the person in the photo? 
2. What is he famous for? 
3. What century do you think this photo is from? 
Students give predictions. 
III. Pre-reading (3 minutes) 
Teacher’s note: 
There are two main ways to write a cause-and-effect essay: 
 focusing on the causes, or 
 focusing on the effects. 
Teacher explains briefly. 
IV. While-reading (10 minutes) 
4.2 Read the text and check your answers. 
Students read silently. 
Teacher helps with difficult sentences if needed. 
V. Post-reading: Understanding structure (5 minutes) 
4.3 Choose the best title for each paragraph 
Correct answers: 
1. d. Early Life 
2. b. A Famous Poet Many Years Ago 
3. c. Later Life 
4. a. A Great Man and Poet 
VI. Pair discussion (3 minutes)

4.4 Check your answers with your partner. 
Students explain why they chose their answers. 
VII. Reading comprehension (5 minutes) 
4.5 Answer the questions: 
1. Where was Jaloluddin born? 
→ In Balkh (Afghanistan, then Persia). 
2. Which poets influenced Jaloluddin? 
→ Attar and Sanai. 
3. Why did he leave home? 
→ Because the Mongols invaded Central Asia. 
4. What helped him to develop as a poet? 
→ Travelling and meeting scholars. 
5. Where was Jaloluddin buried? 
→ Beside his father. 
VIII. Discussion (7 minutes) 
4.6 Discuss the questions:

3. Was travelling important to his work? Why/Why not? 
4. Do you think his friends cared about him? How do you know? 
5. What do these words mean? 
“When we are dead, seek not our tomb in the earth, but find it in the hearts of 
men.” 
Students share opinions. 
IX. Vocabulary work (5 minutes) 
4.7 Vocabulary (English → Tajik) 
(5 words from the text) 
1. poet – шоир 
2. influenced – таъсир гирифта 
3. invaded – ҳуҷум кард 
4. pilgrimage – зиёрат 
5. buried – дафн карда шуд

X. Extension / Homework explanation (2 minutes) 
4.8 Extension (Homework): 
 Choose a Tajik poem. 
 Translate the ideas, not the words, into English. 
 Be ready to explain your choice. 
5. Assessment 
Students are assessed according to: 
 reading comprehension; 
 participation in discussion; 
 correct answers; 
 vocabulary use.

LESSON THIRTY 
GRAMMAR: DRONES 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skill: Grammar, Reading, Writing 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to understand a short text about how 
drones are used in Tajikistan, identify gerunds and infinitives in context, use 
gerunds and infinitives correctly in sentences, and write a short paragraph about 
technology using the correct grammar structures. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Talking about the Image (30.1) 
At the beginning of the lesson the teacher shows an image of a drone being used in 
a mountain area and asks students to describe what is happening in the picture. 
Students explain that the drone is flying above the land and collecting information, 
and they share ideas about why drones are useful in difficult places such as 
mountains or villages at risk from natural disasters. 
III. Pre-reading 
Topic: Predicting the Text (30.2) 
The teacher writes the title “Drones to Find New Homes” on the board and asks 
students what they think the text will be about. Students predict that the text is 
about using modern technology to help people find safer places to live and to avoid 
problems such as floods, landslides, or earthquakes. The teacher briefly explains 
that drones are often used to collect information that people cannot easily get 
themselves. 
IV. Reading 
Topic: Understanding the Text (30.3) 
Students read the text about the village of Basid in Tajikistan and learn that the 
people want to move their village because it is in danger from natural disasters. 
They understand that several international organizations are working together to 
help the villagers plan their relocation and that drones are used to create detailed 
3D maps of the area. These maps help experts choose a safer location for the new 
village. After reading, students answer questions about why the village needs to 
move, how many organizations are involved, and how drones helped with 
planning. 
V. Grammar Focus 
Topic: Gerunds and Infinitives (30.4) 
The teacher explains that gerunds and infinitives are forms of verbs that can be 
used as nouns in sentences. Gerunds end in “-ing” and are formed from the base 
form of the verb, while infinitives are formed using “to” plus the base verb. 
Students learn that both gerunds and infinitives can be used as the subject or object 
of a sentence and that some verbs are followed only by infinitives, some only by 
gerunds, and some by both with or without a change in meaning. The teacher gives 
clear examples related to the text, such as “Using drones helps the villagers” and 
“They want to relocate the village.”

VI. Grammar Practice 
Topic: Choosing the Correct Form (30.5 and 30.6) 
Students complete sentences by choosing between gerunds and infinitives, 
focusing on meaning and grammar rules. They practice common structures such as 
want to, hope to, enjoy doing, suggest doing, and finish doing. The teacher checks 
answers together with the class and explains why each form is correct, helping 
students understand how grammar changes meaning in real situations. 
VII. Writing Activity 
Topic: Technology Helping People (30.7) 
Students choose one type of technology, such as drones, mobile phones, or 
computers, and write a short paragraph explaining how this technology is helping 
people in Tajikistan today and how it may help in the future. They are reminded to 
use both gerunds and infinitives correctly in their writing, for example by 
explaining why using technology is important or how it helps people to solve 
problems. 
VIII. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what they learned about drones 
and grammar, and students briefly explain one new thing they understood. The 
teacher gives short feedback and corrects common mistakes related to gerunds and 
infinitives. 
IX. Homework 
Students write five sentences using gerunds and five sentences using infinitives, 
and they are encouraged to connect their sentences to technology or everyday life 
so that the grammar is meaningful.

LESSON THIRTY-SIX

Time: 45 minutes 
Skill: Speaking 
 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to answer speaking exam questions 
confidently, speak clearly using full sentences, describe personal experiences with 
technology, and participate actively in monologues and group discussions. 
II. Introduction to the Speaking Exam 
Topic: Understanding the Exam Format 
At the beginning of the lesson the teacher explains that this lesson is designed to 
prepare students for the speaking exam. Students learn that the exam has three 
parts, including short personal questions, a monologue, and a group discussion. 
The teacher explains the timing and expectations for each part so that students feel 
more confident and prepared. 
III. Exam Part 1 
Topic: Answering Short Questions (36.1) 
Students are divided into groups of three. Each student answers four questions 
chosen by the teacher. Students are reminded that each answer should be at least 
two full sentences and should include clear ideas and examples. The questions 
focus on how often students use technology, such as the internet, phones, 
computers, and television, as well as their preferences for apps, websites, social 
media, and news sources. While one student speaks, the others listen carefully and 
wait for their turn. 
IV. Practice and Guidance 
During this activity the teacher listens to students’ answers and gives gentle 
guidance. Students are encouraged to speak calmly, avoid one-word answers, and 
explain their ideas clearly. They are also reminded to use simple linking words to 
make their answers sound more natural. 
V. Exam Part 2 
Topic: Monologue (36.2) 
Each student receives one prompt from the teacher and has one minute to prepare 
their answer. Students make short notes and think about the structure of their 
monologue. They then speak for one minute and thirty seconds. Students may 
describe a useful app or website, explain the last time they used a phone and why it 
was important, or talk about a time when they heard or read important news using 
technology. Students are encouraged to speak clearly, stay on topic, and give 
reasons and examples. 
VI. Exam Part 3 
Topic: Group Discussion (36.3) 
Students work together as a group and answer one discussion question given by the 
teacher. They have one minute to respond and must answer as a group, not 
individually. Students discuss how technology connects people, how it can also

GRAMMAR: PERFECT TENSES 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skill: Grammar, Reading, Writing, Speaking 
 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson students will be able to understand a historical text about Martin 
Luther King Jr., identify and use past perfect, present perfect, and future perfect tenses, 
complete grammar exercises correctly, and write short answers using perfect tenses in 
meaningful contexts. 
II. Warm-up 
Topic: Talking about the Picture (39.1) 
At the beginning of the lesson the teacher shows a picture of Martin Luther King Jr. and 
asks students who they think is in the image. Students explain that the person is a famous 
human rights leader and briefly share what they already know about him and his role in 
history. 
III. Pre-reading 
Topic: Predicting the Text (39.2) 
The teacher writes the title “Who was Martin Luther King Jr.?” on the board and asks 
students what they think the text will be about. Students predict that the text will describe 
his life, his work for equal rights, and his importance in history. This activity prepares 
students to understand the main ideas of the reading. 
IV. Reading 
Topic: Understanding the Main Text 
Students read the text about Martin Luther King Jr. and learn that he was an American 
human rights leader who fought peacefully for equality between black people and white 
people. They understand where he was born, how he grew up, what he studied, and how 
peaceful protests influenced his actions. Students also learn about his famous speech, his 
achievements, and the improvements in human rights that have happened since his death, 
while recognizing that the work for equality is not finished yet. 
V. Reading Comprehension 
Topic: Answering Questions (39.3) 
After reading, students answer questions about what Martin Luther King Jr. did, where he

VI. Grammar in Context 
Topic: Completing Sentences from the Text (39.4) 
Students read sentences taken from the text and complete them using words from the box.

historical text. They identify examples of past perfect, present perfect, and future perfect 
forms and understand why each tense is used. 
VII. Grammar Rules 
Topic: Understanding Perfect Tenses (39.5) 
The teacher explains the grammar rules clearly. Students learn that the past perfect uses

“had” with the past participle, the present perfect uses “have” or “has” with the past 
participle, and the future perfect uses “will have” with the past participle. Students 
choose the correct options to complete the rules and check their understanding together. 
VIII. Grammar Practice 
Topic: Choosing the Correct Form (39.6 and 39.7) 
Students complete sentences and a longer text by choosing the correct perfect tense 
forms. They practice using the past perfect to describe actions completed before another 
past action, the present perfect to describe experiences and results connected to the 
present, and the future perfect to describe actions that will be completed by a specific 
time in the future. The teacher checks answers and explains mistakes using examples. 
IX. Controlled Writing 
Topic: Completing Sentences (39.8) 
Students complete sentence starters using correct perfect tense forms. They focus on 
forming correct verb structures and making sentences meaningful and grammatically 
accurate. 
X. Free Writing and Speaking 
Topic: Humanitarians in the Community (39.9) 
Students write a short answer about a person in their community whom they consider to 
be a humanitarian. They explain who the person is and what they have done to help other 
people, using present perfect, past perfect, or future perfect tenses correctly. Students 
then compare their answers with a partner and check each other’s grammar using the 
rules from the lesson. 
XI. Reflection and Assessment 
At the end of the lesson the teacher asks students what new grammar points they learned 
and how perfect tenses help us talk about time more clearly. Students share short 
examples, and the teacher gives brief feedback on grammar use. 
XII. Homework 
Students write a short paragraph about a famous humanitarian or leader. They must use at 
least three examples of perfect tenses and underline them in their writing.

LESSON FIFTY-SEVEN 
GRAMMAR: WISHING 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skill: Grammar, Reading, Speaking, Writing 
 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to identify and correctly use the grammar 
structures “wish” and “if only” to talk about unreal, impossible, or unlikely situations in the 
present and past. Students will also be able to express regrets, desires, and imaginary situations 
in spoken and written English. 
 
II. Introduction to the Topic (New Topic Section) 
In everyday life, people often think about things they want but cannot have, or situations they 
would like to change. In English, we use “wish” and “if only” to talk about these unreal or 
impossible situations. These structures help us express regret, disappointment, or strong desire. 
For example, when something is too expensive, when we don’t have enough time, or when life is 
different from what we want, we can use “wish” or “if only” to explain our feelings clearly. 
 
III. Pre-Reading Activity (57.1 – 57.2) 
Students look at the picture and discuss what is happening in the image. The teacher asks guiding 
questions to help students guess the situation. Then students read the title “If only…!” and 
predict what the text will be about. The teacher explains that the text will describe people talking 
about things they want but cannot have. 
 
IV. Reading the Text (Main Text) 
Students read the text about Person A, B, C, and D carefully. Each person talks about a personal 
wish or regret. The teacher reads aloud or asks students to read silently, focusing on how “wish” 
and “if only” are used in real sentences. 
 
V. Reading Comprehension (57.3) 
Students answer the questions using full sentences. They explain what each person wants and 
why they cannot get what they want. This helps students understand the meaning of the grammar 
in context and connect grammar with real-life situations. 
 
VI. Grammar in Context – Gap Filling (57.4) 
Students complete the sentences using the correct phrases from the box. This activity helps 
students notice sentence structure and how “wish” and “if only” are formed. The teacher checks 
answers together with the class and explains mistakes carefully. 
 
VII. Grammar Rules Explanation (57.5 – Expanded Explanation) 
The teacher explains the grammar rules in simple language. Students learn that “wish” and “if 
only” are used for unreal or impossible situations. The teacher emphasizes that although we talk 
about the present or future, we use past tense forms after “wish” and “if only”. This section is 
very important for understanding English grammar logic. 
 
VIII. Grammar Practice – Choosing the Correct Form (57.6) 
Students choose the correct verb form in each sentence. This activity helps students practice 
using past tense correctly after “wish” and “if only”. The teacher explains why each correct 
answer is used and corrects common errors. 
 
IX. Sentence Completion (57.7) 
Students complete the sentences using their own ideas while following the grammar rules. This 
activity encourages creativity and personal expression while still focusing on correct grammar 
use.

X. Writing and Speaking Task (57.8 – Main Skill Practice) 
Students write a short paragraph answering the given question. They explain something they 
want or would love to have but do not think they will get. Students describe how their life would 
be different if their wish came true. This task combines grammar, writing, and critical thinking. 
Students can then share their answers with a partner and check each other’s grammar. 
 
XI. Summary and Reflection 
The teacher summarizes the lesson by reviewing how “wish” and “if only” are used. Students are 
reminded that these structures express unreal situations and regrets. The teacher may ask 
students to give one example sentence orally. 
 
XII. Homework 
Students write 5 sentences using “wish” and 5 sentences using “if only” about their own life, 
education, work, or dreams for the future.

LESSON SIXTY-TWO 
PRESENTATION: NEGOTIATING 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skill: Speaking, Listening, Presentation 
 
I. Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to express more complex ideas in 
conversation, negotiate politely, explain reasons clearly, and respond appropriately in 
formal and semi-formal situations such as work or financial discussions. 
II. Introduction to the Topic (New Topic Section) 
Negotiating is an important communication skill used in daily life, work, and business. 
People negotiate when they ask for time, money, help, or changes to an agreement. Good 
negotiation requires polite language, clear explanations, respect for the other person, and 
flexibility. In this lesson, students learn how to negotiate calmly and politely through a 
real-life workplace conversation. 
III. Reading and Listening – Main Conversation (62.1) 
Students read and listen to the conversation and focus on how polite language is used to 
negotiate a sensitive topic. The teacher first reads the conversation aloud, then students 
read silently. 
Conversation (completed): 
Mr. Lee: Mr. Campbell, I’m glad to see you. 
I’d like to discuss with you the possibility of getting my salary early. 
Mr. Campbell: Mr. Lee, of course, that’s fine. Could you explain why you need to be 
paid early? 
Mr. Lee: Yes, certainly. My son has been offered a place in the Medical University and I 
would like to pay his registration fees. 
Mr. Campbell: Oh, that’s fantastic. Congratulations! You must be very proud of him. 
Mr. Lee: Absolutely. He’s worked so hard for the last few years and he’s always 
studying. So, it means a lot that he’s been offered a place now. 
Mr. Campbell: That’s excellent. Well, yes, of course we will provide you your salary 
early. Would you like next month’s full salary this week or would you rather have 
maybe fifty percent now and fifty percent at the end of the month? 
Mr. Lee: Fifty percent would be great. The fees are about 5000 somoni and I have a lot of 
it already. I just need an extra 1500 somoni. 
Mr. Campbell: No problem at all. I’ll call our accountant after lunch and let her know. 
She’ll forward the money to you immediately. 
Mr. Lee: Thank you very much for all this. I really appreciate it. 
Mr. Campbell: Not at all. It’s my pleasure. I must say I really am very impressed with 
your son. Congratulations again. 
Mr. Lee: Thank you very much. 
Mr. Campbell: Well, I must get back to work. Do let me know when you get the money 
and if you need anything else. 
Mr. Lee: Of course. Thank you again. Goodbye. 
Mr. Campbell: No problem. Goodbye. 
IV. Understanding the Conversation 
The teacher explains that Mr. Lee is negotiating politely by giving a clear reason, 
showing respect, and accepting a reasonable solution. Mr. Campbell responds 
professionally and offers flexible options, which is an important part of successful 
negotiation. 
V. Personal Reflection Questions (62.2)

VII. Pair Discussion and Negotiation Practice (62.4 – 62.5) 
Students work in pairs with another group that has a different opinion. They politely try 
to convince the other group of their perspective. After that, they switch roles and defend a 
different opinion, practicing flexibility and respectful disagreement. 
VIII. Group Discussion – Personal Opinions (62.6) 
In small groups, students discuss their own views about borrowing money, loaning 
money, and financial responsibility. The teacher encourages students to use polite 
language and examples from real life. 
IX. Lesson Summary 
The teacher summarizes the lesson by highlighting that negotiating is not about arguing, 
but about explaining needs clearly, listening carefully, and finding a solution that works 
for both sides. These skills are useful in work, family life, and society.

LESSON SIXTY-SIX 
GRAMMAR: DIRECT AND INDIRECT SPEECH 
Мавзӯи дарс: 
Гуфтори мустақим ва ғайримустақим (Direct and Indirect Speech) 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skill focus: Grammar, Reading, Writing, Speaking 
 
 
 
Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to identify direct and indirect 
speech, correctly change tenses, pronouns, and word order, and report statements 
and questions accurately. 
Introduction 
People often repeat what others have said. Sometimes we repeat the exact words, 
and sometimes we report the meaning using different grammar. In this lesson, 
students learn how to change direct speech into indirect (reported) speech and 
understand why grammar changes are necessary. 
66.1 Look at the picture 
Students describe what is happening in the picture and guess what people are 
saying to each other. This activates prior knowledge about speaking and reporting 
speech. 
66.2 Read the text and answer the questions 
Students read a personal letter about exams and teachers’ decisions. They answer 
comprehension questions to understand the context and identify examples of 
reported speech in real use. 
66.3 Grammar rules: Direct and Indirect Speech 
Students learn that reported speech is used to repeat something said in the past. 
When reporting, we usually move the tense one step back, change pronouns and 
time expressions, and use normal word order in questions. 
66.4 Grammar transformations 
Students practice changing tenses from direct to indirect speech, including present 
simple to past simple, present continuous to past continuous, past simple to past 
perfect, present perfect to past perfect, and future forms to would or was/were 
going to. Students also practice reporting questions correctly. 
66.5 Grammar in context 
Students read a reply letter and choose the correct grammar forms. This task helps 
them see how reported speech works naturally in real writing, not only in isolated 
sentences. 
Speaking practice 
Students retell what a teacher, doctor, or parent said using indirect speech. This 
improves fluency and confidence in using the grammar orally. 
Conclusion 
Direct and indirect speech are essential for everyday communication. 
Understanding how to report information correctly helps students speak and write 
more clearly and accurately.

Бaёни мавзуи нав: Checking Understanding (8 minutes) 
Checking understanding means making sure that you and the other person 
understand the same thing. In conversations, especially about exams, studies, or 
important decisions, it is normal to ask questions again. This does not mean you 
are weak; it means you are careful and responsible. 
Students learn that checking understanding helps avoid confusion, improves 
learning, and builds confidence. Asking simple questions like “Did you 
understand?” or “In what sense?” can save a lot of problems later.

LESSON SEVENTY-ONE: PRESENTATION: DISCUSSING EXAMS 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skill focus: Speaking, Listening, Critical Thinking 
 
Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to discuss exams confidently, ask for 
advice about tests, express opinions about different question types, and explain 
how to prepare for exams effectively. 
 
Introduction (5 minutes) 
Exams are an important part of school life. Students often feel stressed or unsure 
about how to prepare for tests. Talking about exams, asking teachers for feedback, 
and understanding different types of exam questions can help students feel more 
confident. In this lesson, students practice discussing exams, preparation strategies, 
and expressing opinions about different question types.

 asking politely for help 
 receiving feedback 
 preparing for quizzes and tests 
 importance of clear writing 
 
Бaёни мавзуи нав: Discussing Exams (8 minutes) 
Discussing exams helps students understand what teachers expect and how to 
improve results. When students talk to teachers about tests, they learn how to 
prepare better and avoid surprises. 
This lesson shows that exams are not only about memorizing information, but also 
about expressing ideas clearly, using correct grammar, and organizing thoughts 
logically. Students also learn that long and difficult words are not always 
necessary; clarity is more important than complexity.

Conclusion (5 minutes) 
The teacher reviews the lesson. 
Students explain: 
 why discussing exams is useful 
 how different question types test different skills 
 what study methods work best for them 
The teacher reminds students that preparation, practice, and asking questions are

LESSON SEVENTY-TWO: EXAM PREP 
Time: 45 minutes 
Skill focus: Speaking, Exam Skills, Critical Thinking 
 
Learning Outcomes 
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to answer exam speaking questions 
confidently, give structured monologues about exams, discuss study habits, and 
express clear opinions about exams and exam questions. 
 
Introduction (5 minutes) 
Exams are an important part of education, and students are often asked to talk 
about exams in speaking tests. Knowing how to answer clearly, give examples, and 
organize ideas helps students perform better. In this lesson, students practice 
answering exam questions, giving short monologues, and discussing opinions 
about exams and studying. 
 
72.1 Part 1: Speaking Questions (15 minutes) 
Students work in groups of three. 
Each student answers 4 questions, chosen by the teacher. 
Each answer must be at least two full sentences. 
Questions focus on: 
 easy and difficult subjects 
 study places and study time 
 exam experience 
 study methods and habits 
Students practice speaking clearly, using examples, and giving reasons. 
The teacher listens and gives feedback on clarity and structure. 
 
Baёни мавзӯи нав: Exam Speaking Strategies (5 minutes) 
When answering exam questions, students should not give very short answers. A 
good answer includes an opinion, a reason, and sometimes an example. Speaking 
calmly and confidently is more important than speaking very fast. This lesson 
helps students understand that exams are not only about knowledge, but also about 
communication skills and organization of ideas. 
 
72.2 Part 2: Monologue (10 minutes) 
Each student receives one prompt. 
They have: 
 1 minute to prepare 
 1 minute 30 seconds to speak 
Prompts focus on: 
 describing a real exam experience 
 explaining changes in study habits 
 reflecting on poor exam preparation 
Students are encouraged to speak in order: 
what happened → why → result → lesson learned.

72.3 Part 3: Group Discussion (7 minutes) 
Students answer one group question together. 
They share ideas, agree or disagree politely, and support opinions with reasons. 
Discussion topics include: 
 improving study methods 
 importance of exams 
 quality of exam questions 
 value of essay questions 
The teacher monitors cooperation and participation. 
 
Conclusion (3 minutes) 
The teacher summarizes the lesson. 
Students explain: 
 how they can study better 
 what makes a good exam question 
 why discussion and reflection are useful 
Students are reminded that practice and self-reflection improve exam results.
